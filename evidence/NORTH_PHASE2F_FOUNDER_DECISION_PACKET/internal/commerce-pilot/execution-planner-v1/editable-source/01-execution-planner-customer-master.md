# Start Here

## The 1-3-5 method

The day can be clear without being crowded. Choose **one Big Thing**: the meaningful action that would make the day feel honestly moved forward. Choose **three Medium Tasks**: bounded actions that support the Big Thing or keep essential work moving. Choose **five Small Wins**: small, finishable actions that reduce friction, close loops, or make tomorrow easier.

The method is not a demand to complete nine items. It is a way to see the difference between what matters, what supports it, and what can wait. Some days the Big Thing is a conversation, a decision, a recovery boundary, or a careful first step—not an impressive output.

## Use it in under ten minutes

1. Read your two-week intention before selecting today’s work.
2. Write a specific Big Thing that has a visible definition of done.
3. Add no more than three Medium Tasks. If one cannot reasonably happen today, move it to the parking line rather than pretending.
4. Add up to five Small Wins. They should be small enough to finish without opening a new project.
5. Reserve one realistic focus block. Protecting 25 honest minutes is more useful than planning three imaginary hours.
6. Name the obstacle most likely to interrupt the day, then choose one response you can actually use.
7. At day’s end, mark what happened, write one sentence of evidence, and leave tomorrow a clearer starting point.

## What this is / what this is not

| This is | This is not |
|---|---|
| A short self-guided planning and reflection structure | A diagnosis, treatment, performance rating, or productivity guarantee |
| A way to make one priority visible | A rule that every day must contain nine completed tasks |
| A record of progress, friction, and adjustment | Professional legal, medical, financial, investment, tax, employment, or other advice |
| A tool that can stand alone on paper or screen | A requirement to use the NORTH app or share personal information |

> **Gentle rule:** If a page becomes evidence that you are behind, stop and make the next page smaller. The planner is meant to reduce noise, not manufacture pressure.

# Fictional Worked Example — Jordan’s two-week documentation reset

> This is a fictional workflow example. It does not describe a real person and is not employment, legal, medical, financial, investment, tax, or professional advice.

Jordan coordinates a small internal operations team. Several process notes are scattered across messages and old files. Jordan’s two-week outcome is not “fix every process.” It is: **Create one clear intake guide for recurring requests, test it with one teammate, and note what needs changing.**

**Boundaries:** Jordan will not redesign the entire system, introduce a new software platform, or turn this into an after-hours project. A 45-minute focus block is protected on Tuesday and Thursday mornings.

### Sample day

**Big Thing:** Draft the first version of the intake guide’s “What to include” section.

**Definition of done:** A one-page draft exists in the team’s shared folder with five requested fields and a short example.

**Medium Tasks:**

1. Review three recent request examples and list recurring missing details.
2. Ask one teammate which field they most often need to chase.
3. Add the guide link to the working notes.

**Small Wins:** Rename the draft clearly; close two old request tabs; write the test date on the calendar; put the shared-folder link in the notes; add a parking-line question about approvals.

**Likely obstacle:** Jordan may begin comparing every old request instead of drafting. **Smallest response:** Review only three examples, then begin the field list.

**Evening evidence:** “The guide now has five fields and a small example. One teammate said the ‘needed-by date’ field will help.”

### Sample weekly review

**Keep:** the Tuesday/Thursday focus block and the one-page limit.  
**Change:** ask for feedback earlier rather than waiting for the guide to feel complete.  
**Stop:** reopening old folders for “just one more example.”  
**Next-week choice:** run one real request through the guide and record the unclear field.

# Product-use and accessibility notes

## US Letter and A4 layout review checklist

- [ ] The Start Here section begins on a new page in both layouts.
- [ ] The Two-Week Intention page does not split a field label from its writing area.
- [ ] Each of the fourteen daily pages begins and ends on one page in US Letter and A4 versions.
- [ ] The two reset pages and final review each begin on a new page.
- [ ] Body copy is no smaller than 10.5 pt in print output; labels are no smaller than 9 pt.
- [ ] Contrast is print-safe: deep navy text on off-white / white paper; warm gold is never the only instructional signal.
- [ ] Form fields, if implemented, have visible labels; placeholders alone are not labels.
- [ ] Keyboard order follows the visible page order: date → Big Thing → definition → Medium Tasks → Small Wins → focus block → obstacle → completion → reflection → parking line.
- [ ] Headings use a single logical hierarchy and descriptive section titles.
- [ ] A real PDF/AcroForm implementation, tagged-PDF check, assistive-technology test, and printer proof remain future release blockers.
